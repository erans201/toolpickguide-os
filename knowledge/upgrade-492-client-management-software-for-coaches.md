## What client management software for coaches should actually do in 2026

A sales CRM tracks deals. Coaches don't have deals. They have *relationships that repeat every week.*

Here's the real job description.

### Sell packages, not one-off sessions
Clients should be able to buy a 3-month package, a payment plan, or a subscription, and have their sessions unlock automatically. If you're manually tracking "session 4 of 12" in a spreadsheet, the tool isn't doing its job.

### Run onboarding on autopilot
Contract → payment → intake questionnaire → welcome email. In order. Without you touching it. This is the moment clients decide whether you're a professional or a person with a Calendly link.

### Kill the booking email tennis
"Does Tuesday work?" "How about Thursday?" "Actually, can we do next week?" Scheduling should be one link, synced to your real calendar.

### Give clients a home
A portal with session notes, shared files, homework, and goals. So clients stop asking "where was that worksheet?" and you stop digging through sent emails.

### Let you leave
This one's new, and it matters. More on that next.

## A few "don't mess this up" setup tips (from what goes wrong in real practices)

These are the patterns that trip up coaches again and again. Almost none of them are the software's fault.

**Don't move active clients mid-program.**
Switching someone's portal in week 6 of a 12-week program is changing the locks while they're inside. Put new clients on the new tool and let current programs finish where they started.

**Don't launch with your whole offer menu.**
Build one package first. One offer, one price, one session cadence. Five half-built packages look worse to a prospect than one polished one.

**Don't let onboarding arrive in five emails.**
Contract, payment, intake form, welcome email, calendar link. If each fires from a different tool, clients feel forwarded, not onboarded. Chain them into one sequence, in order, and test it as a client.

**Don't send the intake form *after* the first session.**
It sounds obvious. It happens constantly. If the questionnaire isn't required before booking unlocks, plenty of clients will show up to session one without it.

**Don't skip the export drill.**
Practice.do users learned this the hard way. Export clients, notes, and contracts in week one, just to see what comes out. Then put a quarterly export on your calendar. It takes ten minutes.

**Don't buy a marketing machine you won't drive.**
If your clients come from referrals, funnels and SMS campaigns are a very expensive hobby. Fix the onboarding experience first. That's what generates referrals.

**Don't let your booking page offer every hour you're awake.**
Open availability feels generous. In practice, it means a client books 7am on a Monday, another grabs 8:30pm on a Thursday, and your week turns into a shredded calendar with no deep-work blocks left. Set fixed coaching windows, add buffer time between sessions, and cap sessions per day. Your clients won't notice the limits. Your energy will.

**Don't ignore client caps.**
Simply.Coach Starter is three clients. Delenta Starter is ten portals. A great month can push you into a pricier plan overnight, so check the cap before the launch, not after.

## FAQs (Frequently Asked Questions)

### What is the best client management software for coaches?
For most solo coaches, Paperbell. It bundles packages, scheduling, payments, contracts, and a client portal in one plan at $97/month or $970/year. If goal and progress tracking matter most, choose Simply.Coach.

### Is there a cheap CRM for life coaches?
Yes. Simply.Coach starts at $9/month (billed annually) for 3 clients, and Delenta's Starter plan is $229/year for 10 client portals. Watch the client caps, because both push you up a tier as you grow.

### What happened to Practice.do?
Practice.do shut down permanently on November 3, 2025. Users lost access to portals, scheduling links, and contracts, and unexported data was not recoverable. Paperbell, Simply.Coach, and Delenta are common replacements.

### Can I use HubSpot or Pipedrive as a coaching CRM?
You can, but they're built for sales pipelines, not recurring sessions. You'll still need separate tools for scheduling, packages, and a client portal, which is the duct-tape problem all over again.

### HoneyBook vs Paperbell for coaches?
Paperbell is built for coaching packages and adds no platform transaction fee. HoneyBook is built for multi-service businesses, has stronger proposals, and charges card fees from 2.7% + 10¢. Coaching-only businesses usually fit Paperbell better.

### Do I need GoHighLevel as a coach?
Only if paid marketing, funnels, and SMS drive your growth. For referral-based coaches, it's more machine than you need.
