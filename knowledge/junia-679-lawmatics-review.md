## Marketing automation and email

“Marketing automation” in a law firm should not mean blasting newsletters at everyone. In a sane intake operation, it means two things:

  1. **Follow up with leads who don’t respond right away**
  2. **Stay top-of-mind for leads who aren’t ready yet**

A good automation setup is like an intake assistant who never forgets to follow up, never takes a day off, and never says, “I thought someone else handled that.”

### Deliverability reality: CRM email isn’t the same as Mailchimp

Law firm CRM email lives in a weird middle ground. It’s not purely transactional (like appointment confirmations), and it’s not always purely marketing (like newsletters).

So deliverability matters:

  * domain authentication (SPF, DKIM, DMARC)
  * list hygiene (don’t keep hammering dead leads)
  * content and link practices (avoid spam triggers)
  * consistent sending patterns

Premium and Enterprise list **up to 50,000 email sends/month** on the comparison table. That’s plenty for many firms, but it only matters if those emails land in inboxes.

Ask Lawmatics what’s included in onboarding for deliverability setup, and what your firm needs to do on the domain side.

### 4 automation sequences most firms actually use

Here are sequences that map to real intake outcomes.

**1) “New lead, no answer” sequence (0–7 days)**

  * Immediate “we received your request” confirmation
  * 5-minute task reminder for intake to call
  * If no contact, follow-up email + text (where compliant)
  * Day 2, Day 4, Day 7 attempts and a final “close the loop” message

**2) “Consult scheduled” sequence (pre-consult)**

  * Confirmation + calendar link
  * Reminder 24 hours before
  * Reminder day-of
  * Intake form request with deadlines
  * “What to expect” message that reduces no-shows

**3) “Not ready yet” nurture (30–90 days)**

This is huge in estate planning, family law, immigration, and even PI leads shopping around.

  * educational check-ins
  * FAQ replies
  * “when you’re ready, book here” CTA
  * optional referral prompts

**4) “Reactivation” sequence (old leads)**

  * “Do you still need help with X?”
  * updated availability
  * alternate offer like paid consult (if that’s your model)
  * quick qualification questions

Whether these are included out of the box or need to be built depends on your onboarding package and plan. Ask what templates exist, and what’s custom.

## Reporting and ROI tracking

Most firms don’t have a “lead problem.” They have a “lead visibility” problem.

Lawmatics’ Premium plan card explicitly calls out **custom reporting** , **analytics & ROI tracking**, and **custom dashboards**. That’s the right direction if you’re spending meaningful money on marketing and want to manage it like an operator, not like a gambler.

### Core reporting most firms want

At minimum, firms usually want:

  * leads by source (Google Ads, LSA, referral, organic, etc.)
  * contact rate (did we reach them?)
  * booking rate (did they schedule?)
  * show rate (did they attend?)
  * signed rate (did they retain?)
  * [time-to-first-response](https://www.gorgias.com/blog/customer-support-metrics) (this alone can change conversion rates)
  * [revenue attribution](https://www.netsuite.com/portal/resource/articles/financial-management/revenue-attribution.shtml) (if connected to billing/payment data)

### Attribution limits (don’t over-trust any dashboard)

Even good dashboards can lie by omission.

Problems you’ll run into:

  * phone calls that bypass tracking numbers
  * referrals that get entered inconsistently (“Friend” is not a source)
  * multi-touch journeys (ad today, organic search next week, then call)
  * intake staff selecting the wrong source to move fast

So yes, measure ROI. Just don’t treat attribution as courtroom evidence. Treat it like a compass.

### How to use reports operationally

If you want ROI from Lawmatics, set up a weekly intake meeting around a handful of numbers:

  * median time-to-first-response
  * leads not contacted within 24 hours
  * consults scheduled vs completed
  * signed clients by source (not just leads by source)

Then coach based on reality:

  * If booking rate is low, your scripts and speed need work.
  * If show rate is low, your reminders and pre-consult packet need work.
  * If signed rate is low, the issue might be attorney consult sales process, not intake.

### Questions to ask during the demo

  * “Can we filter reporting by practice area and location?”
  * “Can we export raw data easily?”
  * “Do dashboards support custom fields and custom stages?”
  * “Can scheduled reports go to partners weekly without logging in?”

If reporting is a main reason you’re upgrading, ask them to build a sample dashboard using your real lead sources and pipeline stages.

Don’t accept “it’s available.”

Ask:

  * is it included in our plan price?
  * is it an add-on?
  * is it per user, per message, per contact, or per outcome?

Then have them put that in writing on the quote.

### 4) Decide who will own the system internally

Lawmatics needs an owner. Not forever, but at least for the first 90 days.

Pick someone responsible for:

  * form changes
  * automation edits
  * reporting hygiene
  * staff training

No owner means everyone complains and nobody fixes it.

### 5) Validate integrations with your PMS before signing

If your handoff to Clio/MyCase/PracticePanther is critical, treat integration as a requirement, not a bonus.

Ask for:

  * a live demonstration
  * field mapping examples

## Lawmatics review FAQs

### What is Lawmatics and who is it designed for?

Lawmatics is a legal intake CRM built to help law firms convert more leads into signed clients by streamlining intake, follow-ups, and client management. It is ideal for small and mid-size law firms that rely on speed-to-lead, consistent follow-up, and clean handoffs between marketing, intake coordinators, and attorneys.

### How does Lawmatics pricing work and why isn’t there a public price list?

Lawmatics uses a quote-only pricing model, meaning it does not publish specific dollar amounts for its plans or add-ons. This sales-led approach allows pricing to be tailored based on factors like user minimums, contact volumes, onboarding needs, and support tiers. Pricing details are provided after consultation with the sales team.

### What are the main differences between Lawmatics Essential, Premium, and Enterprise plans?

The Essential plan requires a minimum of 3 users and includes 500 contacts with basic conflict checking and limited automations. Premium also requires 3 users but includes up to 10,000 contacts with advanced conflict checking and unlimited automations. Enterprise requires at least 5 users, includes 10,000 contacts (more sold separately), advanced conflict checking, unlimited automations, and includes Time & Billing.

### Can Lawmatics replace my current practice management system (PMS)?

For most firms, Lawmatics is not a full practice management replacement. While it offers some Time & Billing features (included in Enterprise or as add-ons), many firms continue using dedicated PMS software for matter management, documents, billing workflows, and trust accounting. Lawmatics typically complements PMS by focusing on client intake and CRM automation.

### Who within a law firm typically uses Lawmatics?

Various roles benefit from Lawmatics: Intake coordinators and legal assistants use it for lead tracking and scheduling; marketing coordinators manage attribution and nurture campaigns; office managers or managing partners gain reporting and visibility; attorneys use it for consult scheduling and assessing lead qualification.

### What features does Lawmatics offer to improve the client intake process?

Lawmatics provides tools such as automated lead capture from multiple sources (web forms, calls, ads), conflict checking (basic or advanced depending on plan), task assignments, two-way texting (Premium and Enterprise), online scheduling, e-signatures for retainer agreements, email automation with high monthly send limits, and detailed pipelines to track leads through intake to conversion.
