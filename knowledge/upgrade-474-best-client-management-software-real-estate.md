## What a real estate CRM should actually do in 2026

A generic CRM tracks deals. Real estate is different: long nurture cycles, dozens of lead sources, and deadlines that can sink a closing.

Here's the real job description.

### Call new leads fast, automatically
New lead lands, the right agent gets notified instantly, and a first text goes out while you're still parking the car. Speed to lead is the whole game.

### Know where every lead came from
Zillow, your website, open houses, referrals. If you can't see which source actually closes, you'll keep paying for the wrong one.

### Nurture for months, not days
Many buyers take months to act. Drip campaigns and reminders should keep you top of mind without sounding like a robot.

### Track the transaction, not just the lead
Under contract is where the real deadlines start: inspection, appraisal, financing, closing. Missing one costs more than any CRM subscription.

### Remember past clients
Closing anniversaries, home-value check-ins, referral asks. Your database is the business you'll still have in five years.

### Keep a team accountable
Who called the lead? When? If a manager can't answer that in ten seconds, leads are leaking.

## A few "don't mess this up" setup tips (from what goes wrong in real practices)

These are the patterns that cost agents deals again and again. Almost none of them are the software's fault.

**Don't import your whole phone as "leads."**
Your dentist and your cousin don't belong in a drip campaign. Tag contacts by type before import: past client, sphere, active lead, vendor. Blasting everyone is how you get unsubscribes and spam flags.

**Don't route leads without a response rule.**
Round-robin routing is useless if nobody has to respond. Set a rule: first contact within five minutes, or the lead reassigns. Then check who's actually hitting it.

**Don't text leads before your SMS registration is done.**
Carriers now require [business texting registration](https://www.twilio.com/docs/messaging/compliance/a2p-10dlc). Unregistered messages get filtered or blocked. Finish setup before you import a thousand numbers.

**Don't let "under contract" live in your head.**
Build a transaction checklist with dates: inspection, appraisal, financing, closing. Tie reminders to the contract date, not to your memory.

**Don't forget closed clients.**
Set a past-client plan on day one: closing anniversary, home-value check-in, holiday touch, referral ask. That's your cheapest lead source, and most CRMs never get set up for it.

**Don't sign a 12-month platform contract before a real trial.**
Platforms like Real Geeks and Lofty involve commitments and setup fees. Run a fake lead through the demo, get add-on pricing in writing, and ask what happens to your data if you leave.

**Don't wait for your CRM to disappear.**
LionDesk users got a few months' notice. Export your contacts and notes quarterly, whatever you use. Ten minutes of insurance.

## FAQs (Frequently Asked Questions)

### What is the best client management software for real estate agents?
For most agents, Follow Up Boss: fast lead routing, broad lead-source integrations, and accountability, from $58/user/month billed annually. Solo agents on a budget should look at Wise Agent, which includes transaction management from $42/month billed annually.

### What happened to LionDesk?
Lone Wolf discontinued LionDesk after September 2025 and replaced it with its new "Relationships" CRM. Former users should export any remaining data and choose a replacement. Follow Up Boss and Wise Agent are common picks.

### Is kvCORE the same as BoldTrail?
Yes. kvCORE was rebranded as BoldTrail by Inside Real Estate. Pricing is quote-based, and many agents get access through their brokerage.

### Why don't Lofty and BoldTrail show prices?
Both sell quote-based packages that vary by seats, add-ons, and lead-generation programs. Third-party estimates put entry pricing in the mid-hundreds per month, plus setup fees, but only a written quote is reliable.

### Do I need an IDX website with my CRM?
Only if you generate your own online leads. If your leads come from referrals, Zillow, or your brokerage, a CRM like Follow Up Boss or Wise Agent is enough. IDX platforms like Real Geeks make sense when you're investing in ads.

### What's the cheapest real estate CRM that still works?
Wise Agent at $499/year covers CRM, drip campaigns, and transaction management. Add the SMS add-on if you text leads.
