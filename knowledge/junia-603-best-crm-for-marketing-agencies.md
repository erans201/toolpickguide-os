**Choosing a CRM for marketing agencies** is harder than it looks, because agencies sell twice: once to win the client, then again every retainer renewal.

A decent CRM helps you do both without feeling like you hired a full-time admin. And "best" depends on whether your agency runs on referrals, outbound, partnerships, or a mix of chaos and good intentions.

Generic sales CRMs are built for one-off deals. They track the win, then lose interest, right when your real work (delivery, renewals, upsells) starts.

Per-seat pricing adds up fast, too. A $20 seat looks harmless until you count account managers, ops and the founder who "only logs in on Fridays."

This guide compares seven options for small agencies (2 to 30 people), with verified prices, seat minimums and the catch for each.

A sales CRM is the front door. Agency project tools are the rooms behind it. Some agencies need both, most need to pick which one leads.

## What a marketing agency needs from a CRM

A lot of CRMs are built for product companies. Agencies are different: you have leads, but also proposals, SOWs, renewals, expansions, and the weird "this client is happy but silent" phase.

Here's what actually matters when you pick a CRM for marketing agencies of 2 to 30 people:

- **Lead and deal pipeline:** fast deal creation and simple stages. If adding a lead feels like filing taxes, your team won't do it.
- **Proposals:** a way to track discovery booked, proposal sent, verbal yes and contract out, without custom code.
- **Retainer and renewal tracking:** reminders and one clean view of "clients at risk" and "upsell targets."
- **Client account history:** every email, call and note on the account, so nobody asks the client the same question twice.
- **Handoff to delivery:** what was sold has to reach the people who deliver it, intact.
- **Reporting by client:** which lead source closes, which service line sells, which client is worth growing.
- **Per-seat cost at 5, 10 and 20 people:** price the team you'll have in 12 months, not the team you have today.

## Sales CRM vs agency management software

A CRM is where sales becomes delivery. Your project tool is where delivery becomes results.

Pick a sales CRM (HubSpot, Zoho, monday, Copper or Close) when winning and renewing clients is the bottleneck. Pick Productive when the bottleneck is budgets, capacity and margins after the deal is signed.

If you're small, keep them separate and connect them lightly. Once a client signs, a clear handoff matters more than the tool; our guide to [client onboarding software](/client-onboarding-software/) covers that step.

## How to choose in one afternoon

You don't need a month of demos to choose a CRM for marketing agencies. Four steps get you there:

1. **Count seats for the next 12 months.** Include account managers and ops, not just sales. Then check seat minimums (monday and Productive start at 3).
2. **List the one integration you can't lose.** Google Workspace points to Copper. A HubSpot marketing setup points to HubSpot.
3. **Run one real proposal through the trial.** From first contact to signed contract. Add your [e-signature tools](/best-doc-signing-tools/) and your [form builders for lead capture](/best-form-builders-for-lead-capture/) to see where leads actually enter.
4. **Check what renewals look like.** Can you see which retainers end next quarter in one view? If not, it's a new-business tool only, not a full CRM for marketing agencies.

A simple setup that sticks: one pipeline, two custom fields (lead source and service line), and three automations (a follow-up task 2 days after "proposal sent," an alert when a deal sits still for 14 days, and a kickoff task on "closed won"). Boring pipelines get used.

## Don't mess this up: 5 tips

1. **Promo prices end.** HubSpot's $7 Starter seat is a limited-time new-customer price. Budget for the regular price at renewal.
2. **Seat minimums are real.** monday CRM and Productive both start at 3 seats, even if you're one person.
3. **Contact-based pricing grows with your list.** ActiveCampaign's prices are quoted at 1,000 contacts and rise from there.
4. **Onboarding fees count.** HubSpot Professional adds $1,500 once; Enterprise adds $3,500.
5. **Export your data before the trial ends.** Test the export on day one, so you're never stuck in a tool you didn't choose.

## FAQs (Frequently Asked Questions)

### What is the best CRM for a marketing agency?

For most small agencies that already run marketing in HubSpot, HubSpot Sales Hub is the best CRM for marketing agencies. If budget per seat matters most, Zoho CRM is the value pick, and if outbound is your main channel, look at Close.

### Is HubSpot free for agencies?

Yes, HubSpot has a free CRM with free tools for up to 2 users. Paid Sales Hub starts with Starter at $7/seat/mo paid annually (a limited-time new-customer offer) or $20 paid monthly.

### What's the cheapest CRM for a small agency?

Zoho CRM is free for 3 users, and HubSpot's free plan covers up to 2 users. Among paid plans, Close Solo is $9/user/mo billed annually (1 user only), and Productive Essential is $10/user/mo billed yearly with a 3-seat minimum.

### Do agencies need a CRM and a project management tool?

Many do: the CRM wins and renews the client, and the project tool delivers the work. If you want one tool for both, Productive combines a sales pipeline with budgets and time tracking, and monday CRM keeps sales on the same kind of boards your team may already use.

### Is monday CRM good for agencies?

Yes, if your team likes boards and wants sales and delivery to look the same. Plans start from 3 users, from $12/seat/mo billed annually on Basic; Standard ($17 billed annually) adds 250 automations a month.

### How much does a CRM cost for a 10-person agency?

Multiply the per-seat price by 10 and add any one-time fees. For example, Zoho CRM Standard is $14/user/mo billed annually, monday CRM Standard is $17/seat/mo, and HubSpot Professional is $90/seat/mo plus a one-time $1,500 onboarding fee.
