## What Paperbell is (and who it’s built for)

Paperbell combines a website, scheduling, payments, contracts, and a [client portal](https://toolpickguide.com/client-portal-software/) in a single account, with the pieces already connected. The goal is to reduce the “five tools duct-taped together” setup many coaches end up with.

Paperbell is designed for one service provider per account. If your business model depends on multiple coaches working under one umbrella, that design choice matters.

### Packages, payment plans and subscriptions

Paperbell supports packages with payment plans at any cadence. That includes ongoing pay-as-you-go options and sessions-per-month subscriptions billed until cancellation.

You can also offer pay-in-full discounts, accept deposits, and use coupon codes. This is the heart of Paperbell if your practice is built around structured offers instead of ad hoc sessions.

The “flat plan” pricing model matches this: Paperbell is trying to be the one place you set up your offers and sell them, without pushing you into feature tiers as your client count grows.

### Scheduling and calendar sync

Clients can self-book in their own time zone, and you can add buffer times between appointments. This is one of those small details that prevents the classic “why did you book in the middle of the night my time?” mess.

Paperbell supports two-way sync with Google Calendar, Microsoft Outlook, and Apple iCal. It also supports meeting links for Zoom, Google Meet, or any video tool.

That last part matters because it’s not forcing you into one video platform. It’s more like a universal adapter than a single-brand charger.

### Contracts, forms and surveys

Paperbell includes e-signatures built into the booking flow via Dropbox Sign. It also supports unlimited contracts.

It also supports intake forms, post-session feedback, and check-in surveys. If your onboarding includes more than “pick a time, pay me,” these are the pieces that keep your process consistent.

If you’re still tightening your agreement language, you may also want a separate reference point like this [service contract template](https://toolpickguide.com/service-contract-template/). Paperbell handles the signing flow, but your terms still need to be yours.

### Client portal, notes and content delivery

Paperbell lets clients buy packages and book without a password. That’s intentional friction removal, especially for clients who hate creating yet another login.

Inside the client area, Paperbell includes per-client notes, purchase history, signed contracts, surveys, and appointment history. It’s built to keep the relationship record in one place, tied to the package they bought.

Paperbell also supports delivering PDFs, videos, and worksheets to a package or a single client, including drip delivery over time. Think of it like handing a client a folder, except you can schedule when each document appears.

### Coaching website and offer pages

Paperbell includes a branded coaching site, and it auto-generates a landing page per offer. That’s handy if you want a clean place to send people instead of juggling separate site pages for each package.

Paperbell also supports using your own domain name. For most established coaches, that’s non-negotiable because your domain is your storefront sign, not something you want to rebuild later.

### Group coaching and live classes

Paperbell supports small group coaching, cohort packages, and live online classes. If your business model includes groups, that’s important because it means you’re not forced into 1:1 only.

Packages can mix group and 1:1 sessions. That’s useful for hybrid offers like “weekly group plus one private session per month,” where the structure is the product.

## Payments: Stripe, PayPal and transaction fees

Paperbell routes payments to your own Stripe or PayPal account, and Stripe is recommended for full functionality. Paperbell says it does not hold funds.

Paperbell also states it adds no transaction fee of its own, but Stripe or PayPal will still charge their own processing fees. If you need the details there, use [Stripe’s own processing fees](https://stripe.com/pricing) as the source, since Paperbell doesn’t set those rates.
