# Pixieset Studio Manager Review (2026): Is the Free CRM Enough?

**This Pixieset Studio Manager review** is for photographers wondering if a free CRM can really run their business. Photography CRMs used to be… kinda boring. They were either glorified contact lists, or they were so “powerful” you needed a weekend and a YouTube playlist to set them up.

Pixieset Studio Manager sits in a new middle lane. Pixieset started as the gallery; Studio Manager bolts a CRM onto it. That’s the pitch and the catch.

If you’re already living inside Pixieset Client Gallery, the idea of adding contracts, invoices, and bookings without yet another login is genuinely appealing. The real question is whether the **free plan** is “real CRM free,” or “free until you send your fourth contract.”

## The short verdict

**Great if…**

  * You want a [**free CRM**](https://www.cio.com/article/30668/what-is-crm-customer-relationship-management-definition-benefits-and-examples.html) that covers the basics: booking, contracts, and invoices.
  * You already use **Pixieset Client Gallery** and want your client flow in one place.
  * You want simple workflows and don’t care about deep automation.



**Skip it if…**

  * You need advanced automation or conditional logic (the kind Dubsado is built around).
  * You want client galleries included inside the CRM price (Pixieset separates them).
  * You work with non-photography clients, need multi-user access, or run a team.



## What Pixieset Studio Manager is (and isn’t)

Pixieset is best known for **Client Gallery** , where photographers deliver proofs, sell downloads, and keep client galleries looking polished. Studio Manager is Pixieset’s **CRM add-on** , living inside the same Pixieset account as your galleries, website, and store.

That matters because the experience feels “one ecosystem.” The invoice, contract, questionnaire, and booking tool are designed to fit photography workflows, not generic client work.

But it’s not trying to be everything for everyone. Studio Manager is not a broad, industry-neutral CRM like you’d find in the consulting or agency world. It’s aimed at photographers, and the feature priorities make that obvious.

## Pixieset Studio Manager pricing (2026)

Pixieset has a legitimately usable free tier, but it’s gated in very specific places. Here’s the current lineup.

**Free ($0)**

  * Unlimited invoices
  * **3 contracts** (with e-signatures)
  * **3 quotes or questionnaires**
  * **1 active session type**
  * Basic booking and scheduling
  * Online payments



**Plus ($12/mo billed annually, or $15 month-to-month)**

  * Unlimited quotes and questionnaires

**Pro ($18/mo billed annually, or $24 month-to-month)**

  * Everything in Plus
  * Booking reminders
  * Unlimited session types
  * Collect retainer and tips during booking
  * Manually approve bookings



No setup fees, and you can start without a credit card.

One important footnote: **Pixieset Client Gallery pricing is separate** from Studio Manager. If you’re expecting “CRM + galleries” in one subscription like some competitors bundle, Pixieset doesn’t do that by default.

## What the free plan really gets you (and when you’ll outgrow it)

Pixieset’s free plan is strongest in one specific area: **invoices are unlimited**. That’s not a “trial” feature. If your current mess is “I’m sending invoices in PayPal/Venmo/Stripe links and tracking it in Notes,” the free plan already cleans that up.

Where the free plan clamps down is the “documents and intake” side:

  * **3 contracts total** , with e-signatures.
  * **3 quotes or questionnaires total.**
  * **Only 1 active session type** at a time.



That last one sounds small until you think about how photographers actually sell. If you shoot weddings and families, you now have two different booking offers with different pricing, durations, and questionnaires. On Free, you can only have one live at a time.

So yes, the free plan can absolutely be enough. It just depends on how quickly you’ll hit one of the three ceilings:

  1. contract count, 2) form count, 3) session type count.



A good rule of thumb: if you book more than a handful of jobs per year, you’ll likely step up to Plus. Not because Pixieset breaks, but because the limits start creating friction in the exact moments you want things to feel effortless.

## Features that matter (the ones photographers actually feel day-to-day)

### Booking and scheduling

Studio Manager includes booking and scheduling across plans, including Free. You can publish a booking flow for clients and let them request or lock in dates depending on your plan and settings.

If you’re coming from “DM me and I’ll check my calendar,” this is a real upgrade. If you’re coming from a heavy scheduler like Calendly plus a separate CRM, Studio Manager’s scheduling will feel more “enough” than “endless options.”

### Contracts and e-signatures

Contracts are where Pixieset’s free plan becomes a deliberate teaser (need a starting point? Try our [photography contract template](/photography-contract-template/)). You get **3 contracts with e-signatures** on Free, and then you either stop using Studio Manager for contracts or you upgrade.

For a brand-new photographer, 3 contracts could be a month or it could be a season. For a working photographer, it’s basically a taste.

On Plus and Pro, contracts are unlimited which makes managing contracts much easier. That’s the level where Studio Manager starts to feel like an actual primary CRM instead of a starter kit.

### Invoices and payments

Unlimited invoices on all plans is a big deal. It’s also the most “Pixieset-like” feature decision here, because Pixieset understands photographers are constantly invoicing retainers, balances, add-ons, and print orders.

Online payments are supported, and Pro adds more booking-stage collection options like collecting a retainer and tips during booking. If your workflow is “invoice after consult,” Free can still work. If your workflow is “book now, pay retainer now,” Pro is where Pixieset leans.

### Questionnaires and quotes

On Free, you get **3 quotes or questionnaires**. That limit is similar to contracts: it’s enough to prove the flow, not enough for an active business.

On paid plans, forms are unlimited, which is where Studio Manager becomes useful for things like:

  * lead capture questionnaires
  * wedding detail forms
  * model release style forms
  * post-session feedback



If you want a reference point for what “good” questionnaires look like, here’s a related guide: [photography client questionnaire](/photography-client-questionnaire/).

### Reminders

Pixieset splits reminders by type:

  * **Payment and document reminders** start on Plus.
  * **Booking reminders** are added on Pro.



This is one of those areas where the paid plans reduce the mental overhead. A CRM is partly software, partly “fewer things bouncing around in your head.”

### Branding

Branding starts on Plus. That includes logo, colors, and removing Pixieset branding.

If you care about a premium vibe, branding is not cosmetic. It’s the difference between “you’re booking a professional studio” and “you’re booking someone’s side hustle tool.”

## Where Pixieset Studio Manager falls short (and why it might not matter)

### Automation is light

If Dubsado is a build-your-own machine, Pixieset is more like a tidy toolbelt. You can do the fundamentals, but you won’t get deep, conditional logic workflows that branch based on answers, packages, or client types.

If your dream is: “If they choose Package A, send Email Set A, assign Questionnaire A, generate Invoice A, and delay Contract A until payment clears,” Pixieset won’t scratch that itch the same way.

### It’s photographer-focused, not team-focused

Studio Manager is built for photographers, not multi-role teams. If you’re running a studio with multiple shooters, assistants, and admin help, you’ll want to look carefully at user access and collaboration expectations.

Pixieset’s design feels like it assumes a single owner-operator, which is most photographers, but not all.

### Client Gallery pricing is separate

This is the one that catches people. If you assumed “Pixieset Studio Manager includes galleries,” it doesn’t.

To be fair, Pixieset’s gallery product is mature and deep on its own. But from a budgeting perspective, it means you’re comparing “CRM pricing” plus “gallery pricing” versus competitors that bundle more into one monthly number.

## Pixieset Studio Manager vs Studio Ninja and Dubsado

This is the comparison many photographers actually need. The day-to-day decision tends to look like this: do you want a simpler photography CRM, or a heavier automation-first system?

Think of it like cars. Pixieset Studio Manager is a reliable daily driver with a clean dash. Dubsado is a project car with endless modifications if you’re willing to tune it.

### Pixieset Studio Manager vs Studio Ninja

**Studio Ninja** is a photography CRM that’s built around the core pipeline: lead, booking, invoices, contracts, questionnaires, job tracking. It’s a specialist, and it tends to feel like “CRM first.”

Pixieset Studio Manager feels like “Pixieset first.” If you already use Pixieset for galleries, Studio Manager is naturally convenient. One account, one brand system, less glue-work between tools.

Here’s the practical trade:

  * **Pixieset wins** if you want a free starting point and like the idea of keeping client-facing pieces inside Pixieset’s ecosystem. It’s also attractive if you want fewer tools and fewer integrations to maintain.
  * **Studio Ninja wins** if you’re shopping for a CRM as the “source of truth” for your whole business and you want something that’s deeper than Pixieset’s light automation approach. It’s also often preferred by photographers who want job tracking discipline baked in.



The catch: Pixieset’s free plan is a real wedge here.

Studio Ninja starts at $16/mo ($160/yr) for 1 user and 5 active jobs, and the Starter plan lacks booking forms and automation. Pro is $27/mo ($270/yr) for 3 users and unlimited jobs, and Master is $40/mo ($400/yr). There’s a 7-day trial with no card needed.

**Source:** [Studio Ninja’s official pricing page (checked October 2026)](https://www.studioninja.co/pricing/)

### Pixieset Studio Manager vs Dubsado

**Dubsado** is still the poster child for customization and workflow logic in photography CRMs. It’s the system people pick when they want their CRM to behave like a well-trained assistant.

Pixieset Studio Manager is the opposite philosophy. It’s more guided and more minimal. That’s great when you want to set it up in an afternoon, and frustrating when you want the system to do 12 things automatically.

Where Pixieset tends to win:

  * You want a **simpler setup** and fewer decisions.
  * You want a **free option** that is not a time-limited trial.
  * You already use Pixieset and like the unified ecosystem.



Where Dubsado tends to win:

  * You want conditional workflows and deep automation.
  * You want heavy customization of forms, proposals, and pipelines.
  * You want to build different client experiences for different services without hitting “session type” style limits.



On price, Dubsado Starter is $35/mo ($335/yr) with 1 active lead capture form and no scheduling or workflows. Premier is $55/mo ($525/yr) and adds scheduling and automated workflows. The trial runs 21 days with full Premier access and no card needed.

**Source:** [Dubsado’s official pricing page (checked October 2026)](https://www.dubsado.com/pricing)

A subtle point: Dubsado can be “too much CRM” for some photographers. If you don’t enjoy fiddling with systems, Pixieset’s constraints can actually feel like relief.

### Quick cheat sheet

  * Choose **Pixieset Studio Manager** if you want basic CRM features, minimal setup, and a strong free tier, especially if you already use Pixieset galleries.
  * Choose **Studio Ninja** if you want a photography CRM that’s deeper than Pixieset, with more of a CRM-first mindset.
  * Choose **Dubsado** if your business needs automation logic and customization more than it needs simplicity.



## Who should use it

Pixieset Studio Manager makes the most sense for a specific kind of photographer. For the wider field, see the [best client management software for photographers](/client-management-software-for-photographers/). If you’re in that group, the “is free enough?” question becomes easier.

### 1) Photographers already using Pixieset Client Gallery

This is the cleanest fit. You’re already in the Pixieset ecosystem, so Studio Manager is a natural extension rather than another tool to integrate.

You’re basically saying: “I already deliver work here. Can I also book and get paid here?” For a lot of photographers, that’s the whole point.

### 2) Newer photographers who need a real system, not another spreadsheet

The free plan is good for building the habit. You can standardize invoices immediately, and you can run a few real bookings through contracts and forms before you commit money.

This matters because many photographers don’t need “the best CRM.” They need “a CRM they’ll actually use.”

### 3) Low-volume, high-touch photographers

If you shoot fewer sessions per month and you do a lot of relationship-driven work, you might not need automation. You need a clean way to send a contract, invoice, and questionnaire without chaos.

Pixieset Studio Manager covers that job well, particularly once you step up to Plus.

### 4) Photographers who hate fiddling with workflows

Some CRMs reward tinkering. Pixieset rewards you for not tinkering.

If your ideal admin day is “send, book, invoice, done,” Pixieset’s simpler approach will feel like a feature, not a limitation.

### Who should probably skip it

  * Photographers with multiple service lines who need advanced branching workflows.
  * Teams or studios needing multi-user functionality and robust internal collaboration.
  * Anyone who wants galleries included in their CRM subscription price and doesn’t want to pay Pixieset separately for that side.



## Don’t mess this up: 5 tips

Pixieset Studio Manager is easy to start, but it’s also easy to set up in a way that quietly creates friction. These are the five mistakes that tend to show up later.

### 1) Treat the free plan like a “production trial,” not your forever setup

The free plan is great, but it has hard limits. If you burn your 3 contracts and 3 forms on half-baked drafts, you’ll hit the ceiling before you’ve even built your process.

Write your core contract and questionnaire intentionally first. Then use the free plan to run real client work through it.

### 2) Use the 1 session type limit strategically

On Free, you get **one active session type**. So pick the offer that represents most of your bookings right now, not the offer you hope to sell next year.

If you split your business (mini sessions, families, couples, weddings), you’ll outgrow Free quickly. That’s not a failure, it’s just the pricing design.

### 3) Don’t underprice the “branding” upgrade

Removing Pixieset branding and matching your brand colors sounds superficial until you watch a client go from your Instagram to your booking page. Consistency is part of trust.

If your work is premium-priced, Plus is often worth it just to keep the client journey feeling like one brand.

### 4) Decide whether you want “invoice-led” or “booking-led” payments

Some photographers prefer booking first, then invoice. Others want payment (or at least the retainer) as part of booking.

Pixieset can do both, but the smoothest “collect retainer during booking” flow is a Pro feature. Pick the flow you want first, then choose the plan that supports it without workarounds.

### 5) Don’t expect Dubsado-level automations, and you’ll be happier

This sounds obvious, but it’s the #1 disappointment pattern. People hear “CRM” and assume it will behave like a highly automated workflow engine.

Go into Pixieset expecting a clean, simple system with light automation. If you truly need conditional workflows, you’ll know within a week, and you should just choose Dubsado.

## FAQs (Frequently Asked Questions)

## Is Pixieset Studio Manager really free?

Yes. The **Free** plan is $0 and doesn’t require a credit card to start, and it includes unlimited invoices plus limited contracts and forms.

The “gotcha” is not hidden fees. It’s that the free plan is capped at **3 contracts** , **3 quotes/questionnaires** , and **1 active session type**.

## Is the free plan enough to replace HoneyBook or Dubsado?

For some photographers, yes, especially if you do low volume and want the basics. If your workflow needs automation, conditional logic, or lots of different service offerings at once, the free plan will feel cramped quickly.

Even Pixieset’s paid plans are more “simple CRM” than “automation machine,” which is where Dubsado tends to shine.

## Does Studio Manager include Pixieset Client Gallery?

No. **Client Gallery is priced separately** from Studio Manager. Studio Manager is an add-on style product inside the Pixieset account, but the gallery subscription is its own line item.

Gallery plans run from a free tier (3 GB) to $40/mo billed annually for Ultimate.

## What happens when I use my 3 free contracts?

You’ll need to upgrade to Plus (or Pro) for unlimited contracts, or stop using Studio Manager for contracts. Realistically, if you like the tool, this is one of the first limits that nudges you into a paid plan.

## Can I take online payments through Pixieset Studio Manager?

Yes. Online payments are supported, and invoices are unlimited on all plans.

Pro adds more booking-stage payment collection options, such as collecting a retainer during booking. Offline payments with conditional workflows are not a strong suit of Pixieset Studio Manager.

## Does Pixieset Studio Manager have automations?

It has some reminders on paid plans (payment and document reminders on Plus, booking reminders on Pro). But if by “automation” you mean deep, conditional workflows, Pixieset is light compared to tools like Dubsado.

## Is Pixieset Studio Manager only for photographers?

It’s built for photographers and that’s clearly the focus. If you’re a non-photography creative or you need features designed for broader client service businesses, Studio Manager may feel too specialized.

## Pixieset Studio Manager vs HoneyBook: which is better?

It depends on what you mean by “better.” Pixieset Studio Manager is compelling if you want a free or low-cost photography-focused CRM and you already like Pixieset’s ecosystem.

HoneyBook is a general-purpose clientflow platform, and its Essentials plan adds automations. It also costs more: $29/mo to $109/mo billed annually, and it has no client galleries.

## The bottom line: is the free CRM enough?

Our Pixieset Studio Manager review comes down to this: the free plan is enough to run a simple photography workflow, especially if you’re early-stage or low-volume. Unlimited invoices plus basic booking is not “fake free.”

The limits are real, though. Once you need more than one session type or you’re booking consistently and sending contracts weekly, you’ll outgrow Free fast, and Plus becomes the practical baseline.

If you want a simple system that lives next to your Pixieset galleries, Studio Manager makes a lot of sense. If you want your CRM to behave like a programmable assistant, you’re still shopping in Dubsado territory.
