## Why people leave Dubsado

The biggest reason is the learning curve. Dubsado can feel like a mini-CRM builder: forms, workflows and templates all take real setup time. That power is real, but so is the time cost, especially if you're a solo photographer or coach juggling delivery, sales, and admin.

The second reason is price, because Dubsado's "decision point" hits fast. **Starter is $35/month** and **Premier is $55/month** (billed monthly). Many small businesses start on Starter, then realize the features that make Dubsado feel modern are on Premier.

Those Premier-only features matter: **scheduling, automated workflows, public proposals, Zapier integration, and bookkeeping integration**. If your current flow depends on those, Starter isn't really $35/month for you. Your real number is $55/month billed monthly, and that's before you count the time spent maintaining workflows.

That hidden cost is the quiet one. If you're spending Sunday afternoons fixing workflows, that's not automation. That's unpaid operations work, and a simpler system that's "good enough" can win even if the sticker price is similar.

## The 6 best alternatives to Dubsado

## Best pick by business type

This is the part most people skip when comparing Dubsado alternatives, then regret the purchase. Pick based on how you sell and deliver, not on how pretty the dashboard looks.

A quick way to decide is a 30-minute trial test. Create a fake inquiry, send yourself a proposal, sign it, pay an invoice (or simulate payment), then look at everything as if you were the client.

Red flags during trials: a clunky client experience, weak template control, missing payment methods you rely on, and "customization" that's either too limited or so deep it becomes a second job.

### Photographers

Most photographers need booking plus a clean contract and payment flow, with minimal back-and-forth. If that's you, start with **HoneyBook** as the closest swap, or **Moxie** if you want cheaper and simpler.

For a wider comparison, see our guide to [client management software for photographers](https://toolpickguide.com/client-management-software-for-photographers/). Also, don't treat contracts like boilerplate, because your workflow tool won't save you from a bad agreement. Start from a solid [photography contract template](https://toolpickguide.com/photography-contract-template/) you understand.

### Coaches

Coaches usually win by reducing friction: package checkout, scheduling, reminders, and a place to keep the relationship organized. That's why **Paperbell** is the cleanest pick if your business is built around sessions and packages.

If you're a coach who also runs a small team, or you deliver structured projects (like a 12-week program with deliverables), **Plutio** can make sense because it blends client flow and delivery. Just make sure you're not buying project management you won't use.

### Designers and other creative freelancers

Designers tend to care about scope clarity, approvals, milestones, and payment schedules. **Bonsai** is a strong fit when you want structured proposals, contracts and invoices, and a consistent system your future subcontractor can follow. **Plutio** works well if you also want project boards in the same place.

If you're solo and mostly want "send the proposal, sign the contract, get paid," **Moxie** is the simpler and cheaper path. Whatever you pick, a clear [proposal template](https://toolpickguide.com/proposal-template/) does more for your close rate than any software feature.

## Who should stay on Dubsado

Not everyone should switch. Dubsado alternatives make sense when the setup or the bill is the problem, but some businesses are better off where they are.

Stay on Dubsado if your business genuinely benefits from deep workflows and automation that spans a lot of edge cases. If your client types vary a lot and your process changes based on form answers, Dubsado is one of the better "build it your way" systems.

Also stay if you already paid the setup cost. If your templates, workflows, and scheduler are dialed in, switching can be like repainting your studio because you got tired of the light switch placement. Sometimes the smarter move is to simplify what you already built.

Two more groups should think twice. Multi-brand businesses get extra brands for $10/month each, which is hard to beat. And teams of up to 4 users (you plus 3 additional users free) pay no extra per seat, unlike Bonsai's per-user plans.

## Don't mess this up: 5 tips before you switch

  1. **Export your clients and templates first.** Download your client list and copy your proposal, contract, invoice and form templates before you touch automations. Getting paid on time beats fancy workflow logic.
  2. **Run the new tool's trial on one real client.** New leads go to the new system, active clients finish in the old one. That one rule prevents most awkward client emails.
  3. **Check your contract and invoice templates carry over.** Send yourself the link, sign it on your phone, pay from a personal card, and read every email. Ask, "Would this confuse my client at 10pm?" If yes, it's not ready.
  4. **Time the switch to your billing date.** Cancel Dubsado right before it renews, so you don't pay for a month or year you won't use. Annual plans make this timing matter more.
  5. **Count users and active clients against plan limits.** Bonsai charges per user and Plutio Core stops at 9 active clients. Check the limits before the trial ends, not after the first invoice.
