## What client management software for photographers should actually do in 2026

A generic CRM tracks sales deals. It won't hold a wedding date, split a retainer from the final payment, or send a shot-list questionnaire six weeks before the big day.

- **Book fast:** inquiry → proposal → contract → retainer, on the client's phone, in minutes.
- **Tie contract and retainer together:** a signed contract with no retainer is just a very polite maybe.
- **Schedule questionnaires off the shoot date,** not whenever you remember on a Sunday night.
- **Deliver galleries,** or link cleanly to the gallery tool you already use.
- **Split payments backward from the event date,** and chase late ones politely.
- **Stay calm in peak season.** If it needs daily babysitting in October, it's the wrong tool.

## A few "don't mess this up" setup tips (from what goes wrong in real practices)

**Don't migrate during wedding season.**
Switching tools in June is changing lenses mid-ceremony. Move in the quiet months, and let booked weddings finish in the old system.

**Don't separate the contract from the retainer.**
If clients can sign without paying, some will. Then your date is held by someone who hasn't committed.

**Don't schedule questionnaires by calendar date.**
Tie them to the shoot date: timeline six weeks out, shot list three weeks out, final details one week out.

**Don't build payment plans that end on the wedding day.**
Final balances should land two to four weeks before. Chasing money at the reception is not a vibe.

**Don't open unlimited booking slots.**
Cap sessions per day and add buffer time. A booking page that accepts twelve back-to-back mini sessions will happily ruin your Saturday.

**Don't skip the export drill.**
Táve users just lived through a platform change. Export clients, contracts, and invoices in week one, then quarterly.

## FAQs (Frequently Asked Questions)

### What is the best client management software for photographers?
For most solo photographers, HoneyBook: proposals, contracts, and payments in one polished flow. Want galleries in the same tool? Sprout Studio. Starting free? Pixieset Studio Manager. Studios with associates should look at VSCO Workspace.

### Is there a free CRM for photographers?
Yes. Pixieset Studio Manager has a free plan with unlimited invoices, online payments, and a booking site, capped at 3 contracts and 3 quotes or questionnaires. VSCO also offers a free basic Workspace.

### What happened to Táve?
VSCO acquired Táve in May 2025 and rebranded it as VSCO Workspace in August 2025. Full Workspace now comes with VSCO One ($499.99/year), and a free basic tier exists.

### Which photography CRM includes client galleries?
Sprout Studio includes galleries and album proofing on every plan, and VSCO One includes unlimited galleries. Pixieset pairs Studio Manager with its own gallery product. HoneyBook and Dubsado don't host galleries.

### What's the cheapest paid option?
Pixieset Studio Manager Pro at $18/month billed annually, then Studio Ninja Pro at $270/year. Compare payment processing fees too, since they often outweigh the subscription.

### HoneyBook or Dubsado for photographers?
HoneyBook is faster to set up and more polished out of the box. Dubsado offers deeper customization for less per year, but its best features are Premier-only and take real setup time.
