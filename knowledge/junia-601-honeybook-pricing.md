**HoneyBook pricing** looks simple: three plans, starting at $29 a month.

And if all you want is a neat number for your budget, you could stop there. But that's not what most service businesses end up paying.

The catch is billing frequency (monthly vs annual) and payment processing fees. HoneyBook pricing also depends on plan limits that only matter once you use them daily, like automations, team members and lead forms.

This guide covers every plan, what monthly vs annual billing really costs, the payment fees, the free trial, and when a cheaper tool makes more sense. It's written for photographers, coaches, designers and planners who are close to buying.

## HoneyBook pricing plans at a glance (2026)

Every plan includes unlimited clients and projects. The differences are automation, scheduling, team size and lead forms.

- **Starter: $29/mo billed annually, $36/mo billed monthly.** Invoices and payments, proposals and contracts, calendar, all templates, client portal, basic reports, HoneyBook AI, up to 2 live lead forms, and a scheduler limited to 1 session with email-only reminders.
- **Essentials ("Most Popular"): $49/mo billed annually, $59/mo billed monthly.** Everything in Starter, plus the full scheduler (unlimited sessions, email and SMS reminders), automations, QuickBooks Online integration, up to 2 team members, up to 10 live lead forms, standard reports, and the option to remove "Powered by HoneyBook."
- **Premium: $109/mo billed annually, $129/mo billed monthly.** Everything in Essentials, plus unlimited team members, priority support, multiple companies, advanced reports, unlimited live lead forms, and an onboarding specialist (1:1 setup in the first 90 days).

The pricing page lists no paid seat add-ons. HoneyBook also offers free file setup: its team turns your existing documents into templates.

**Source:** [HoneyBook's official pricing page](https://www.honeybook.com/pricing) (checked October 2026)

## Starter: what you get for $29 a month

Starter is for people who want to stop living in their inbox but don't need automation yet. You get the core clientflow: proposals, contracts, invoices, payments and a client portal, with unlimited clients and projects.

It fits newer businesses taking a few clients a month: a photographer doing mini sessions, a coach selling single sessions, a designer with simple project scopes. If you need a starting point for your paperwork, our [photography contract template](/photography-contract-template/) and [client intake form template](/client-intake-form-template/) pair well with it.

Three limits push people to upgrade:

- **No automations.** Every follow-up and reminder is manual.
- **Up to 2 live lead forms.** Fine for one offer, tight for several.
- **A scheduler limited to 1 session with email-only reminders.** No SMS reminders.

Starter is often enough to prove HoneyBook works for you. Many businesses outgrow it once they standardize onboarding.

## Essentials: the plan most businesses land on

Essentials is where HoneyBook starts saving time you can feel every week. Automations plus templates mean fewer "just checking in" emails and fewer missed steps.

What it adds over Starter:

- **Automations** for follow-ups, reminders and next steps.
- **The full scheduler:** unlimited sessions, with email and SMS reminders.
- **QuickBooks Online integration** for your books.
- **Up to 2 team members**, such as a second shooter or an assistant.
- **Up to 10 live lead forms** for different services.
- **Remove "Powered by HoneyBook"** from client-facing pages.

If your brand is premium, that last one matters. It's like arriving at a luxury venue on a spare tire: it still works, but people notice.

Essentials fits wedding photographers, planners, and coaches running packages or retainers. ## Premium: when it's worth $109 a month

Premium is for businesses where clientflow is a system, not just a tool. It adds unlimited team members, multiple companies, advanced reports, unlimited live lead forms and priority support.

It also includes an onboarding specialist for 1:1 setup in your first 90 days. And if you process more than $500,000 a year in transactions, Premium can qualify you for discounted card fees.

Who shouldn't buy it: if you're not using automations heavily or you're still figuring out your process, Premium is expensive potential energy. It looks powerful, but you pay for capability you aren't turning into saved time.

## Monthly vs annual billing: how much you save

HoneyBook shows a "per month" price even when you're billed yearly. The annual toggle reads "Billed yearly (save up to 18%)."

Annual billing is like buying the season pass: cheaper per ride, but only if you're sure you'll keep riding. Here's the yearly math from HoneyBook's own prices:

| Plan | Monthly billing × 12 | Annual billing × 12 | Yearly difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Starter | $432 | $348 | $84 |
| Essentials | $708 | $588 | $120 |
| Premium | $1,548 | $1,308 | $240 |

Monthly billing is basically renting. It costs more, but it buys flexibility while you find out whether HoneyBook fits.

## HoneyBook payment processing fees

If clients pay you through HoneyBook, you pay processing fees. That's true of every payment platform, but you should know the rates before you price your services.

- **Cards:** HoneyBook's pricing page says card fees start at 2.7% + 10¢. The same page's plan comparison table says fees start at 2.9% + 25¢. Confirm the rate in your own account before you set your prices.
- **ACH bank transfer:** 1.5%.
- **Instant deposit:** a 1% fee, pending eligibility.
- **Disputes:** a $10 dispute fee, charged by Stripe ([HoneyBook's payments glossary](https://help.honeybook.com/en/articles/9472957-glossary-finance-and-payments)).
- **International:** no international or premium card brand fees, and no extra fees for international clients.

![A client paying a small business by card at the counter, the moment HoneyBook pricing fees apply](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1556742049-0cfed4f6a45d?auto=format&fit=crop&w=1600&q=60)

A quick example: on a $2,000 invoice paid by ACH, 1.5% comes to $30. If you book high-ticket packages, nudging clients toward bank transfer is the simplest way to keep more of each payment.

Think of it like catering. The menu price is one thing; the service charge is another. Build the fees into your prices instead of being surprised by them.

## Is there a HoneyBook free trial (and a money-back guarantee)?

Yes. HoneyBook is free for 30 days with no credit card required. HoneyBook notes "minor limitations based on eligibility for some advanced features" during the trial.

The trial doesn't roll into a paid plan automatically, so you won't get charged by surprise ([HoneyBook's billing FAQ](https://help.honeybook.com/en/articles/7020217-top-questions-answered-about-honeybook-billing-and-membership)).

After you pay, there's a 60-day money-back guarantee: a full refund if you're not happy after the first 60 days.

## Is HoneyBook worth the price?

HoneyBook pricing makes sense when the tool replaces several others. Here's the honest list.

**Pros:**

- Unlimited clients and projects on every plan.
- Free file setup: HoneyBook's team turns your documents into templates.
- One client record, one paper trail and one portal, instead of a duct-taped mix of tools.
- A 30-day free trial and a 60-day money-back guarantee.

**Cons:**

- Team size is capped: Essentials includes up to 2 team members, and only Premium is unlimited.
- Processing fees scale with your revenue.
- No automations on Starter, so the cheapest plan does the least work for you.

### Which plan fits your business

**Photographers:** Starter is fine for occasional sessions where you mainly need contracts and invoices in one place. For weddings or multi-step shoots, Essentials is the sweet spot, because you'll use templates and automations constantly.

**Coaches and consultants:** Starter can work if you sell single sessions and the scheduler's 1-session limit is enough. Once you sell packages with a consistent onboarding, Essentials is where the manual follow-ups stop.

**Designers and creatives:** branding, web builds and retainers have phases and milestones, so Essentials tends to fit best. If a project manager or VA needs access too, count heads: more than 2 team members means Premium.

**Planners and event pros:** you live and die by process, so Essentials is the practical minimum. Premium earns its price when you run many parallel projects, a team, or more than one company.

If you handle more than a few clients a month, the unified system is the value. It's like buying a home gym: the monthly cost is low, but the real question is whether you'll use it.

## Don't mess this up: 5 tips before you pay

1. **Pick annual only after the trial.** Use the 30 free days first; annual billing only saves money if you stay.
2. **Count team members before choosing Essentials.** It includes up to 2; more than that means Premium.
3. **Price your services with processing fees included.** Check your card rate in your account, since the pricing page shows two different figures.
4. **Use the free file setup.** Hand HoneyBook your contracts and proposals so your templates are ready when the trial starts.
5. **Export data from your old tool before the trial clock starts.** And put your renewal date in your calendar 30 days ahead, so you renew based on usage, not hope.
