## W2-1 · Quora · Which law practice management software do law firms use?
**URL:** https://www.quora.com/Which-law-practice-management-software-do-law-firms-use
**Why this one:** 44 followers; the existing answers are long feature lists, none explains how firms actually decide.

The honest answer is that it splits by firm size more than by quality.

- **Solo and small firms (1–10 lawyers):** mostly Clio, MyCase, PracticePanther, and CosmoLex if they want legal accounting built in. All cloud, all monthly per-user pricing.
- **Mid-size firms:** Clio and MyCase still, plus tools like Filevine or Smokeball when a practice area needs heavier document automation.
- **Large firms:** usually an enterprise system plus a separate document management platform, chosen by IT rather than by the partners.

What actually decides it inside a firm is rarely the feature list. It's three things:

1. **Trust accounting.** If the software makes reconciliation painful, the bookkeeper will veto it. Ask for a demo of a full month-end trust reconciliation, not a slideshow.
2. **Adoption.** The tool the least technical person in the office will open every day beats the "more powerful" one nobody uses.
3. **Leaving.** Ask how you'd export every matter, document and time entry if you switch later. Firms that skip this question get stuck.

If you're choosing now, write down the one thing that breaks most often in your week (missed deadlines, slow billing, intake chaos) and demo only the two tools that are strongest at that.

## W2-2 · Quora · How can small law firms implement effective practice management software?
**URL:** https://www.quora.com/unanswered/How-can-small-law-firms-implement-effective-practice-management-software
**Why this one:** unanswered, and it's the question people ask after buying, where most rollouts fail.

Most rollouts fail in the first month, not because of the software, but because the firm tries to move everything at once. What works for small firms:

**1. Pick one owner.** One person (often the office manager, not a partner) owns the rollout and makes the small decisions. Committees stall.

**2. Move active matters only.** Bring over open matters, contacts and trust balances. Leave closed files in the old system or an archive. You can import history later if you ever need it.

**3. Start with three habits, not thirty features.** Week one: every new matter is opened in the system, every call or email gets logged to a matter, every hour is tracked there. Nothing else yet.

**4. Get trust accounting right before you go live.** Reconcile the opening trust balances to the penny with your bookkeeper, and have someone who knows your state bar's rules check the setup.

**5. Add one feature a week.** Document templates, then the client portal, then online payments, then intake forms. Each one only after the last one is used daily.

**6. Book a 30-day review.** What are people still doing outside the system? That list is your next week's training.

Most vendors offer free onboarding sessions. Use them, and bring your real files, not the demo data.

## W2-3 · Quora · What's the best transaction management software for real estate?
**URL:** https://www.quora.com/Whats-the-best-transaction-management-software-for-real-estate
**Why this one:** 38 followers; existing answers are 3+ years old and miss today's options and pricing.

It depends on who's doing the transaction work, because the tools are built for different people:

- **An individual agent:** Dotloop is the common pick. As of October 2026 it's free for up to 10 transactions, then $34.99/month for Premium.
- **A transaction coordinator or a team:** Open to Close is built around the coordinator's checklist and automations (from $99/month per user, 30-day free trial).
- **A brokerage that lives on compliance review:** SkySlope, priced for brokerages (starts at $340/month).
- **A brokerage that wants simple and predictable:** Paperless Pipeline charges by transaction volume with unlimited users (from $69/month for up to 5 transactions a month).
- **Tight budget:** DocJacket has a free plan for 2 active transactions.
- **You'd rather have one system:** Wise Agent includes transaction management inside its CRM.

The test that matters: set up one real deal from contract to close in the free trial. Every deadline should hang off the contract date, so when closing moves, the whole checklist moves with it. If you're re-typing dates by hand, it's the wrong tool.

And check what your brokerage already pays for before you buy anything. Many brokers provide one, and running two is how documents get lost.
