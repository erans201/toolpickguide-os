# Capsule CRM Review (2026): Simple CRM, Honest Limits

The catch is in the packaging. Capsule charges per user, workflow automations only start on the Growth plan, and the free plan stops at 250 contacts and 2 users.

This Capsule CRM review covers every plan and what it costs per user, billed monthly or annually. Then the features that matter for a small service business, the pros and cons, who should skip it, and five tips before you pay.

## Capsule CRM review: the short verdict

Capsule is a good fit for small teams that want contacts, a sales pipeline and simple projects working this week, not next quarter. It's clean, it's quick to learn, and there are zero setup or onboarding fees.

It's a weaker fit if you need heavy automation on a tight budget. Workflow automations start on Growth at $36 per user per month billed annually, so the cheap Starter plan is a manual-process plan.

Think of Capsule as a well-organized filing cabinet with a calendar on the side. It won't run your sales process for you, but your team will actually use it.

## What Capsule CRM is (and who it's built for)

Capsule keeps your contacts, sales pipelines, projects, tasks and calendar in one shared place. Every call, email and next step sits on the contact record, so nobody has to ask "who talked to this client last?"

If you're still fuzzy on the category itself, here's a plain-English guide to [what client relationship management software is](/what-is-client-relationship-management-software/). Short version: one shared memory for your client relationships.

Capsule's own plan labels tell you who it's aimed at:

- **Starter:** "Perfect for businesses with one to five employees."
- **Growth:** for small businesses with sales teams (Capsule marks it "Recommended").
- **Advanced:** "For bigger sales teams, with more than one salesperson."
- **Ultimate:** "Ideal for scaling teams with 5+ users."

Notice what's missing: there's no enterprise tier with a sales call to get a quote. Every plan has a listed price, which is refreshing.

The sweet spot is consultants, agencies, accountants, freelancers and small B2B teams. People who sell a service, juggle a handful of open deals, and then have to deliver the work.

## The free plan: what 250 contacts and 2 users get you

Capsule's free plan isn't a time-limited trial in disguise. It's a permanent plan with tight walls.

Here's what you get for $0:

- Up to 250 contacts
- A maximum of 2 users
- 5 custom fields
- 1 sales pipeline
- A single project board
- Custom sales milestones
- Sending email from your Gmail or Outlook account

What you don't get: AI features and automations. Capsule's own page is upfront that those need a paid plan.

So who is it for? A solo consultant with a modest client list, or two partners who want to stop tracking deals in a spreadsheet. If you have 250 contacts today, though, you'll hit the ceiling fast.

Think of it as a studio apartment. Perfect for one, workable for two, and the moment a third person moves in, everyone's looking at listings.

## Starter vs Growth vs Advanced vs Ultimate

| Plan | Annual billing (per user / month) | Monthly billing (per user / month) | Contacts | Key additions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Starter | $18 | $22 | 30,000 | Email templates, shared mailbox, basic reporting, premium integrations, goals, AI pipeline generator, custom activity types |
| Growth | $36 | $40 | 60,000 | Workflow automations, advanced sales reporting, dashboards, multiple pipelines and project boards, email sync, team and access controls, AI enrichment, AI summaries, AI meeting prep (beta) |
| Advanced | $54 | $60 | 120,000 | 50 project boards, 50 sales pipelines |
| Ultimate | $72 | $75 | Not stated | Your own account manager, custom training |

**Starter** is the step up from free that most small teams take first. You get 30,000 contacts, email templates, a shared mailbox for addresses like sales@ or support@, basic reporting, and premium integrations with Xero, Zendesk and Import2.

What Starter doesn't have is automation or more than one pipeline. That's fine if you run one sales process and someone on the team enjoys clicking "next step."

**Growth** is where Capsule turns from organized to helpful. Workflow automations, multiple sales pipelines, multiple project boards, email sync, reporting dashboards and team and access controls all arrive here, plus most of the AI features.

If you sell two kinds of service (say, one-off projects and monthly retainers), Growth is the plan that lets you keep them in separate pipelines.

**Advanced** doubles the contact limit to 120,000 and allows 50 project boards and 50 sales pipelines. It's built for bigger sales teams with more than one salesperson, where pipelines multiply by region, service or rep.

**Ultimate** adds your own account manager and custom training. Capsule's sales team also gets in touch after a trial sign-up. It's for scaling teams with 5+ users who want a person to call, not just a help center.

## Monthly vs annual billing: what you save per user

Capsule's pricing toggle says "Pay Annually (Save up to 18%)." The FAQ on the same page says annual plans get "up to 15% discount."

Two percentages on one page isn't a scandal, but it's a good reason to ignore both and look at dollars. Here's year one, per user:

| Plan | Monthly billing × 12 | Annual billing × 12 | You save per user |
|---|---|---|---|
| Starter | $264 | $216 | $48 |
| Growth | $480 | $432 | $48 |
| Advanced | $720 | $648 | $72 |
| Ultimate | $900 | $864 | $36 |

Now multiply by heads. A team of 3 users on Growth billed annually pays $1,296 a year.

Per-user pricing is like paying for gym memberships: cheap for one, but count heads before you sign the whole team up. The per-user saving looks small, and it grows with every seat you add.

My take: start monthly during your first weeks, then switch to annual once the team is actually logging activity. Annual billing only saves money on a tool people use.

## Features that matter for a small service business

This part of our Capsule CRM review sticks to the features that move the needle for a small team, with the plan that includes each one. If a feature isn't listed, don't assume it's there.

### Contacts and sales pipelines

Contacts are the heart of Capsule. Each record holds the person or company, your history with them, and what happens next, and you can import contacts and opportunities from spreadsheets.

Pipelines scale with the plan:

- **Free:** 1 sales pipeline, custom sales milestones, 5 custom fields.
- **Starter:** still one pipeline, plus custom activity types, goals and the AI pipeline generator.
- **Growth:** multiple sales pipelines.
- **Advanced:** up to 50 sales pipelines.

Start with one pipeline. One. Most small service businesses that build five pipelines on day one end up maintaining a museum.

### Projects and tasks

Capsule isn't only a sales tool. Once a deal is won, you can track the delivery work as a project, with tasks and dates on the calendar next to your sales follow-ups.

Project boards follow the same ladder as pipelines: a single project board on Free and Starter, multiple project boards on Growth, and 50 project boards on Advanced.

For an agency or consultant, that's the useful part. The client, the deal and the work live in one place instead of three tabs.

### Email templates, shared mailbox and email sync

Email support grows in three steps:

- **Free:** send email from your Gmail or Outlook account.
- **Starter:** email templates and a shared mailbox (for addresses like sales@ or support@).
- **Growth:** email sync, so conversations land on the right contact without copy-pasting.

The shared mailbox on Starter is underrated. If two people answer the same inbox, it stops the classic "I thought you replied" problem.

### Workflow automations and reports

Workflow automations are a Growth feature. That's the single biggest reason teams move up from Starter.

Reporting follows the same split. Starter has basic reporting, while Growth adds advanced sales reporting and reporting dashboards.

Growth also brings team and access controls. Useful the day you hire someone who shouldn't see every client record.

### Capsule AI

The AI pipeline generator arrives on Starter. The rest of Capsule's AI sits on Growth and up:

- **AI business enrichment** and **AI contact enrichment**
- **AI summaries** of the last 50 activities on a contact, opportunity or project
- **AI meeting prep** (in beta)

The summaries are the practical one. Before a call, you get the gist of a long client history instead of scrolling through it.

On privacy, Capsule says it never uses your data to train AI models, and data in AI features isn't shared outside your account. You can also switch AI features off.

## Integrations and the Marketing add-on

Capsule lists 60+ integrations, plus Zapier for anything not on the list. The most popular ones are Transpond, Mailchimp, Xero, QuickBooks, Gmail and Office 365.

Premium integrations with Xero, Zendesk and Import2 start on the Starter plan. If your accountant lives in Xero, that's a reason to skip the free plan.

**Source:** [Capsule's integrations page](https://capsulecrm.com/integrations/) (checked October 2026)

The Marketing add-on is Transpond, an email marketing suite owned by Capsule. It covers email marketing, webforms, marketing analytics and automations, starting from $11 per month, and you activate it from within your Capsule account.

That's handy if you want newsletters and lead forms feeding straight into your CRM. Just budget it as a separate line, on top of the per-user plan.

## Don't mess this up: 5 tips before you pay

1. **Count users first.** Pricing is per user, so the real number is price × people × 12. A team of 3 on Growth billed annually is $1,296 a year, not $36.
2. **Check your contact count.** Free stops at 250, Starter at 30,000, Growth at 60,000 and Advanced at 120,000. Export your list and count before you pick.
3. **Trial Growth if you need automations.** Starter has none. Use the free 14-day trial on Growth, with your team added, and see whether you'd actually miss them.
4. **Pick annual only after the trial.** Annual saves $48 per user in year one on Starter or Growth. That saving only matters if the team keeps using it.
5. **Know the downgrade rule.** You can downgrade any time, but you must fit the smaller plan's limits. Capsule's example: going from Growth with 60,000 contacts to Starter means removing 30,000 contacts.

If you take one thing from this Capsule CRM review, take tip 1. Most CRM budget surprises are a headcount problem, not a price problem.

## FAQs About Capsule CRM

### Is Capsule CRM free?

Yes, there's a free plan for $0. It's limited to 250 contacts, a maximum of 2 users, 5 custom fields, 1 sales pipeline and a single project board, with no AI features or automations.

### How much does Capsule CRM cost per user?

Starter is $18 per user per month billed annually ($22 billed monthly). Growth is $36 ($40), Advanced is $54 ($60) and Ultimate is $72 ($75), all per user per month in USD.

### Does Capsule CRM have a free trial?

Yes. Every paid plan has a free 14-day trial with no card required. You get all the features of the plan, and at the end you can continue, switch plans or cancel.

### Which Capsule plan includes workflow automations?

Growth and above. Starter doesn't include workflow automations, and neither does the free plan.

### How much do you save with annual billing?

Per user in year one, annual billing saves $48 on Starter, $48 on Growth, $72 on Advanced and $36 on Ultimate. Capsule's toggle says "save up to 18%," while its FAQ says "up to 15%."

### Is Capsule CRM good for small businesses?

For small service teams that want a simple CRM with projects built in, yes. If you need heavy automation on a tight budget, note that automations start on Growth at $36 per user per month billed annually.
