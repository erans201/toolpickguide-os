## W4-1 · Quora · Can you recommend a good law office management software?
**URL:** https://www.quora.com/Can-you-recommend-a-good-law-office-management-software
**Why this one:** a broad legal buyer question; search snippets show old answers with prices that are years out of date. Clio vs MyCase is the shortlist most small firms end up with.
**Angle:** count what you actually buy (one platform vs two products) before comparing prices.
**Later bridge (not this week):** `/clio-vs-mycase/`

For a small firm, the shortlist usually ends up as Clio or MyCase. Both are solid. The difference that surprises people is **how many products you end up paying for.**

- **MyCase** is one platform with three plans. As of October 2026, Basic is $50 per user per month billed annually ($60 monthly), Pro is $100 and Advanced is $130 (billed annually). Client intake forms and the legal CRM start at Pro, so if intake matters to you, Pro is your real starting price.
- **Clio** splits the job in two: Clio Manage runs matters, time, billing and trust; Clio Grow handles intake and leads. Clio Manage lists a starting price of $49 per user per month (early October 2026). If you want the intake side too, price both products together.

So compare like with like. Write down the three things you need on day one (say: matters and billing, trust accounting, online intake) and ask each vendor for the price of **exactly that**, per user, per year. The headline number rarely matches what a firm ends up paying.

Then two quick checks during the trial:

1. **Run one real matter end to end:** intake, conflict check, engagement letter, first invoice, payment. Count how many screens and logins it takes.
2. **Ask how you'd export everything** (matters, documents, trust ledgers) if you leave in three years. A clear answer is a good sign.

And run the trust-accounting setup past your state bar's rules, not just the vendor's sales rep.

## W4-2 · Quora · What is the easiest CRM in real estate?
**URL:** https://www.quora.com/What-is-the-easiest-CRM-in-real-estate
**Why this one:** "easiest" is a real buyer worry (agents abandon CRMs); snippets show tool lists without saying what makes one easy.
**Angle:** easy = the one you open every morning; simple CRM vs all-in-one website platform.
**Later bridge (not this week):** `/follow-up-boss-vs-lofty/`

"Easy" in a real estate CRM means one thing: **you open it every morning and it tells you who to call today.** If it can't do that from your phone in under a minute, it isn't easy, no matter how nice the demo looked.

Two very different kinds of CRM get called easy:

- **A focused follow-up CRM.** Follow Up Boss is the one agents usually mean. It pulls in leads from wherever they come from, and its Action Plans send the follow-up for you. As of October 2026, Grow is $58 per user per month billed annually ($69 monthly), with a 14-day free trial and no contract. Easy because it does one job.
- **An all-in-one platform.** Lofty bundles an IDX website, the CRM and its Smart Plans automation in every package. Easy in a different way: one login for the site and the leads. The catch: Lofty doesn't publish prices (every package is quote-based as of October 2026), so get the full yearly cost in writing, including any lead-gen fees.

How to tell which "easy" is yours:

- **You already have a website and lead sources** → a focused CRM is simpler. Fewer things to set up.
- **You have no site and want leads coming in from one place** → an all-in-one can be simpler, if the price works.

Before you pick, export your contacts from your phone and email into one spreadsheet. If the CRM's import makes that painful in the trial, it will be painful forever. And set up just **one** automated follow-up plan in week one. Agents who try to build ten on day one are the ones who quit.

## W4-3 · Quora · What is the best CRM for a marketing agency in 2026?
**URL:** https://www.quora.com/What-is-the-best-CRM-for-a-marketing-agency-in-2026
**Why this one:** exact match for our live agency page and a current-year question; snippets show answers that each push one tool (some from vendors' own Spaces).
**Angle:** agencies run two pipelines (winning clients and delivering work); pick by which one hurts.
**Bridge (this week):** `/best-crm-for-marketing-agencies/`

Most agencies actually need two pipelines, and the "best CRM" depends on which one is hurting:

1. **New business:** leads, proposals, follow-ups, won and lost deals.
2. **Delivery:** retainers, hours, budgets, which client is eating the team's week.

If deals slip through the cracks, you need a sales CRM. If you win work but margins vanish, you need agency operations software with a light CRM attached. Buying a sales CRM to fix a delivery problem is a common mistake.

Rough map (prices as of October 2026):

- **You already run marketing in HubSpot:** stay there. HubSpot's free CRM covers up to 2 users. Watch the jump to Professional ($90 per seat per month billed annually), which also has a required one-time $1,500 onboarding fee.
- **Small team on a budget:** Zoho CRM is free for 3 users; Standard is $14 per user per month billed annually.
- **The pain is delivery, not sales:** Productive.io starts at $10 per user per month billed yearly (3-seat minimum) and covers budgets, resource planning and time tracking, with clients free to add.
- **A visual board your account managers will actually update:** monday CRM, from $12 per seat per month billed annually on Basic.

Whatever you shortlist, run the same test: put your last five real deals and your two busiest retainers in it during the trial. If the team still reports status in Slack instead of the tool, keep looking.

## W4-4 · Quora · What should a basic freelance contract include to avoid disputes?
**URL:** https://www.quora.com/What-should-a-basic-freelance-contract-include-to-avoid-disputes
**Why this one:** cross-vertical (agencies, coaches, consultants); snippets show clause lists without saying which dispute each clause prevents.
**Angle:** map each clause to the fight it prevents; one lawyer review of the master template.
**Later bridge (not this week):** `/service-contract-template/`

The easiest way to build one: think of the fights freelancers actually have, and give each one a clause.

- **"That's not what I asked for."** → **Scope and deliverables.** List exactly what you deliver, in what format, by when. Also list what's *not* included. That one line saves more arguments than anything else.
- **"Can you just add…"** → **Revisions and change requests.** How many rounds are included, and how extra work is quoted and approved (in writing, before you start it).
- **"We'll pay when it's live."** → **Payment terms.** Deposit up front, the schedule for the rest, when invoices are due, and what happens if they're late (for example, you pause work).
- **"Is it done?"** → **Acceptance.** How the client signs off, and what happens if they go quiet (say, deemed accepted after a set number of days).
- **"We're cancelling."** → **Termination.** Either side can end it with notice; the client pays for work done to date.
- **"We own everything, even your templates."** → **Ownership.** Rights in the final work transfer when you're paid in full; your own tools, templates and know-how stay yours.
- **Plus the basics:** confidentiality, that you're an independent contractor, and which state's law applies.

Two habits matter as much as the wording:

1. **Get it signed before any work starts**, together with the deposit. A contract signed halfway through is a negotiation.
2. **Keep one master template**, have a lawyer in your state review it once, and reuse it. Then each project only needs a new scope section.

## W4-5 · Quora · As a professional photographer, what business software do you use to track and invoice clients, and pay taxes?
**URL:** https://www.quora.com/As-a-professional-photographer-what-business-software-do-you-use-to-track-and-invoice-clients-and-pay-taxes
**Why this one:** photographer workflow question that mixes three jobs; snippets show tool lists that don't separate client admin from bookkeeping.
**Angle:** three jobs, three layers (client tool, bookkeeping, tax pro), connected by one integration.
**Later bridge (not this week):** `/honeybook-pricing/`

The question bundles three different jobs, and most photographers get into trouble by expecting one tool to do all three:

1. **Track and invoice clients** (inquiries, contracts, retainers, payment schedules): a client management tool for creatives.
2. **Bookkeeping** (income, expenses, mileage, gear purchases): accounting software.
3. **Taxes:** a CPA or tax preparer, fed by clean books from step 2.

The trick is making 1 talk to 2, so every payment lands in your books without re-typing.

For the client side, HoneyBook is a common pick. As of October 2026 it's $29 per month billed annually on Starter ($36 monthly), which covers proposals, contracts, invoices and payments. The QuickBooks Online integration starts on Essentials, $49 per month billed annually. That integration is the bridge to job 2. Other photographers use Dubsado, Studio Ninja or Pixieset Studio Manager for the same role; check whether your pick connects to the accounting tool you (or your CPA) already use.

Two money details people miss:

- **Payment fees are a real expense.** HoneyBook lists ACH bank transfers at 1.5%, and card fees starting at 2.7% + 10¢ (its plan table also shows 2.9% + 25¢, so confirm in your account). On big wedding balances, nudging clients toward bank transfer adds up.
- **Keep business and personal money apart:** one business bank account and card, everything runs through them. That alone makes tax season far easier.

For the tax part itself (quarterly estimates, deductions, sales tax on prints), ask a CPA who works with photographers or creatives. That's not a software decision.

## W4-6 · Quora · What tools do you use for organizing your coaching work?
**URL:** https://www.quora.com/What-tools-do-you-use-for-organizing-your-coaching-work-1
**Why this one:** coaching is maintain-only (§4), but this is an open "what do you use" question that matches our coaching page; snippets show single-tool answers.
**Angle:** start with a three-piece stack; the signal to switch to an all-in-one is re-typing, not tool count.
**Later bridge (not this week):** `/client-management-software-for-coaches/`

Most coaches start with three pieces, and that's fine:

- **A booking link** for sessions (with reminders).
- **A payment tool** for packages or invoices.
- **A notes system:** one document or note per client, with the date, what you covered and the agreed actions.

What matters more than the tools is the habit around notes: **write them within a day of the session**, and put the client's next actions somewhere they'll see them (an email recap works). That's what clients remember you for.

The signal to move to an all-in-one coaching tool isn't "I have too many apps." It's **re-typing**: copying a client's name and package into three places, checking a spreadsheet to see how many sessions they have left, chasing a contract by email. When that eats an hour a week, consolidate.

At that point, coaching-specific platforms put booking, payments, contracts, intake forms, session counts and notes under one login. Paperbell is one built around packages ($97 per month or $970 per year as of October 2026). If you send custom proposals instead of fixed packages, a general client management tool like HoneyBook or Dubsado fits better.

Whatever you use, two habits:

1. **One onboarding sequence:** agreement → payment → intake form → first session link. Same order, every client.
2. **Export your client list and notes every quarter**, so years of client history never get stuck in one app.
